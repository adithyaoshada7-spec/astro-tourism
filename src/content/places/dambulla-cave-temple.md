## Overview
The Dambulla Cave Temple, also known as the Golden Temple of Dambulla, is a magnificent cave complex and a revered Buddhist site in Sri Lanka. A UNESCO World Heritage site, it holds the distinction of being the largest and best-preserved cave temple complex in the country. The history of this sacred place dates back to the 1st century BCE, when King Valagamba sought refuge here. Over centuries, subsequent kings enriched the caves with intricate murals and numerous Buddha statues, transforming them into a breathtaking repository of Buddhist art and iconography.

Situated at the heart of the Cultural Triangle, Dambulla is the quintessential sister destination to the legendary [Sigiriya Ancient Rock Fortress](/places/sigiriya), located barely 20 kilometers north. Together with southern heritage landmarks like [Galle Fort](/places/galle-fort), it forms an essential stop on any cultural pilgrimage through Sri Lanka. The complex comprises five main caves, each adorned with vibrant frescoes depicting the life of Buddha, significant moments in Buddhist history, and various deities. Below the cave temples, the modern Golden Temple complex stands, marked by its colossal golden Buddha statue and serving as a museum providing insights into the site's rich history.

## Top Things to Do & See
- **Explore the Five Main Caves:** Each cave has its unique charm and collection of statues and murals. Don't miss the 'Cave of the Divine King' (Devaraja Lena) with its 14-meter reclining Buddha, and the 'Cave of the Great Kings' (Maharaja Lena), which houses thousands of colorful murals.
- **Admire the Frescoes:** Take your time to appreciate the detailed frescoes depicting Jataka tales (stories of Buddha's previous lives), significant historical events, and mythological scenes.
- **Visit the Golden Temple and Museum:** Before or after climbing to the caves, explore the modern Golden Temple at the base, which features a massive golden Buddha and a museum detailing the site's history and Buddhist art.
- **Enjoy Panoramic Views of Sigiriya:** From the high rocky terrace of the cave complex, enjoy sweeping panoramic views across the valley, where you can spot the majestic silhouette of [Sigiriya Rock Fortress](/places/sigiriya) rising on the horizon.
- **Observe Wildlife:** Keep an eye out for playful macaques along the stairway to the caves; remember not to feed or touch them.

## Insider Local Travel Tips
- **Perfect Day Trip Pairing:** Combine your visit with [Sigiriya](/places/sigiriya) in a single day — tackle the Sigiriya rock climb early in the morning, rest over a traditional rice-and-curry lunch, and visit Dambulla in the gentler late-afternoon light.
- **Dress Modestly:** As a sacred Buddhist site, shoulders and knees must be covered. You'll also need to remove your shoes before entering the temple caves, so wear comfortable footwear that's easy to slip on and off (socks are recommended for hot stone pavings).
- **Visit Early or Late:** To avoid the midday heat and the largest crowds, plan your visit for early morning (around 7-8 AM) or late afternoon (after 3 PM). The light for photography is also better during these times.
- **Hire a Guide:** While not strictly necessary, an authorized local guide can significantly enhance your experience by providing historical context and explaining the intricate details of the murals and statues.
- **Bring Water:** The climb to the caves involves a moderate ascent of about 150 meters, so carry sufficient drinking water.
